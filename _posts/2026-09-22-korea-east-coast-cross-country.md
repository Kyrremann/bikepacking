I've made it a yearly tradition to go bikepacking, but when I started planning this year's trip, Japan didn't quite scratch the itch.
So I decided that it was time for me to travel with my bike to a new country.
Korea has been on my map for quite some time, as I quite enjoy Korean cinema, and have friends who can recommend this beautiful country.

Since they have their own map services, and Google Maps isn't as up to date as other countries, I had a hard time doing research for my trip.
So instead of "banging my head against the wall", I decided to try out a more loose approach for this trip.
I just had the two stamp routes I wanted to do, and the rest would be explored while I rode.

This worked pretty well, even though I ended up with longer days in the saddle, and fewer museums than I had wished for.
Still, I learned a lot about the long history of Korea, something I didn't know about at all.

## Verdict (TL;DR)

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261001_130048.jpg)
*The beginning of October turned out to be a great month to look at flowers in bloom.*

My first trip to Korea couldn't have gone better.
The weather was beautiful, starting with 30 degrees the first week, and a more pleasant 23 degrees at the end.
The landscape was picturesque, and the bike roads felt safe, but a bit monotone.

Planning for Korea wasn't as easy, as they use Naver and Kakao Maps instead of Google Maps.
They both have street view, so you can look to see if roads are good for riding.

Camping in Korea was really easy, as there were plenty of Koreans camping near the beach, or the many rivers.
So I mostly just copied them, and pitched my tent in the same area.
There are also toilets everywhere, but showers were a bit harder to find, so be prepared with wet wipes.
Finding hotels last minute wasn't really a problem either.
All of them had a place to store my bike too, which is really nice.

If you are new to bikepacking, Korea is a great place to start.
The cross-country route is probably the easiest (of the long ones), but if you like to see the ocean, and in my view, a more exciting trip, the East coast is the place to go.
For a shorter trip you could take the train to Chuncheon, and cycle back to Seoul.

## The route

![](https://kyrremann-bikepacking.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/korea/map-of-my-trip.jpg)
[My route (on RideWithGPS)](https://ridewithgps.com/collections/10455804)

While planning the route, I had a couple of different suggestions for what to ride.
I ended up going with the most straight forward route, starting in Seoul, heading over the mountains to the East coast.
Then I did the East coast stamp route, before starting in Busan to do the Cross-country stamp route up to Incheon.
I even had time to visit the Ganghwa Peace Observatory to take a look at North Korea.

Both of the stamp routes where made using maps from [KoreaByBike.com](https://koreabybike.com/) and searching on Ride With GPS.
The route connecting Seoul and the East coast was made by looking at the gravel routes from [KoreaEpicRide.kr](https://www.koreaepicride.kr/).
Even thought I only had one day with gravel, it was a good way to find places to ride.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260924_095358.jpg)
*The gravel road was of good quality, but also really steep.*

For parts of the route going from the peace observatory, and to the East coast, I followed what I believe was the DMZ Peace Trail.
If I could plan it again I would look more into that, because the parts I rode were really nice.

## Transportation

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261009_152709.jpg)
*Packing down my bike at the end of my trip.*

As usual, I took my bike with me on my flight from Norway.
I had booked the same hotel (Glad Mapo) before and after my cycle trip, and in advance asked if I could store my luggage there while I was out cycling.
This worked really well, and I was also allowed to pack my bike at the end of my trip in my hotel room.
It is so much nicer to pack in the room, than on the street.

While on my bike trip I ended up taking the bus one time.
From Yeongdeok, the end of the East coast route, to Busan.
I first tried to take the train, but it only allowed foldable bikes, or I would've had to pack it in a bike bag.
So my guess is that, similarly to in Japan, using a rinko bag would've allowed me to bring my bike onto the train.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261004_143127.jpg)
*Sadly the express bus wasn't as cool as this little bus.*

Most of my trip I used Naver to look for museums and camping grounds.
I did the same when trying to find a bus to Busan, but the two buses I ended up taking were not a suggestion on Naver.
It may have been because they were express buses, and that you also end up "outside" of Busan city center.
My bus trip went from Yeongdeok bus station (영덕터미널), switching buses at Pohang, and then finally arriving at Nopo aka Busan Central Bus Terminal.

Actually taking the bus was easy, got tickets from the ticket window, and just had to put my bike underneath the bus.
The first bus driver rolled his eyes a bit when I saw me, but didn't say anything.
Luckily there was plenty of space.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261003_150125.jpg)
*The bike museum, although small, was well worth a visit.*

## Accommodation

My goal was to camp every night, but I also knew realistically that that wouldn't happen.
I tried different types of accommodations, from sleeping next to the river, camping grounds, hostels, and apartment hotels.
Before I came to Korea I thought wild camping was illegal, but people were camping everywhere, especially close to the beach.
So now I'm not so sure, there were also some parks with terraces that people camped on.
I tried one of them, but setting up a tent without pegs is not the easiest job.
So I prefer the ground.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261003_061819.jpg)
*This was maybe the campground I had my best night of sleep, for some reason.*

One thing I like to do after riding all day is to wash my body, but as in Norway, this wasn't always feasible.
The campgrounds always had showers, but some of them required reservation the day before.
In one of them I was allowed to take a shower for free, which was all I actually need from a campground.

When I had hotels, keeping my bike safe was never really a problem.
Either I could keep it in my room, or it was stored in the lobby.
As in Japan, I was never afraid of someone stealing my bike or gear.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260928_143426.jpg)
*There were toilets all over the place!*

## What I'm happy I did

The best part of the trip was cycling in the mountains between Chuncheon and the coast.
It felt wild, and away from tourists.
It was also here I tried a proper forest road.
Turned out to be a bit too hard for my liking, especially for the time I was willing to use.
Though, it was a fun experience.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260922_175443.jpg)
*Along the route you will cycle on several of these walk ways!*

Before I came to Korea I wasn't sure if I wanted to do the stamp rally, but I'm really glad I did.
I like collecting stuff, and the passport made collecting the stamps fun, especially with the badge and medal.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260925_160402.jpg)
*The stamps are in the old phone booth, but not every booth had an ink pad, so bring your own.*

I'm also really happy that I got time at the end to look at North Korea.
I had hoped to visit the border before starting the East coast route, but you had to have a car or be in a bus to be allowed in.
So then, at the end being able to cycle to the Ganghwa Peace Observatory was really interesting.
On my way to the observatory I had to pass through two checkpoints where I had to show my passport.
As I took a different route on my way back, I just passed one of the checkpoints, so I guess not every road had the same checkpoints.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260925_100246.jpg)
*This is as far north I managed to ride, there was a military checkpoint next.*

## Challenges

Cycling in Korea has been very easy.
As I went for the stamp routes, everything kinda was facilitated for a good time.
The main issue I have with the stamp route, especially the Cross Country route, is that it goes a tad bit far away from restaurants, cafés, and convenience stores.
Meaning that there wasn't as many places to casually stop for snacks or an ice cream.
So I had to plan a little bit more when I wanted to eat, and also buy extra snacks when I stopped (not really a problem).

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261007_123418.jpg)
*This was one of the best meals on the trip, from 조안하우스, right next to the route.*

The routes are very scenic, but it's not that many sightseeing spots.
So finding museums was not straight forward.

Another challenge was understanding what kind of food was served at restaurants.
Turns out I know too little about Korean food, so it was always a little work to understand the menu, and what food I would get.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261007_103917.jpg)
*Korea also had a lot of cute mascots!*

Lastly, getting a shower every night wasn't always that easy, so I got to practice my skill of using wet wipes.
While eating at a pizzeria/surf shop on the East coast route, I got to talk with a family, and they gave me a tip about asking surf shops for a shower.
As they usually have showers for their surfers.
I never got to try the tip my self, as the family asked the owners before I could.
So that was yet another night where I could go to bed in my sleeping bag freshly showered.
A really good feeling!

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260928_071832.jpg)
*It was a challenge to pitch my tent on the terrace, need some new poles for wood.*

## What would I change for my next trip

As I'm already thinking that I would like to finish the stamp routes, I don't think I have any big changes.
The bike lanes can be monotone, but listening to podcasts along the way really helps.
	
![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261001_143257.jpg)
*Even though they could be monotone, I can't deny how beautiful they were.*

So the biggest hurdle on the trip was food, so I need to learn more about Korean food.
And maybe see if there are better ways to clean myself than with wet wipes in lieu of a shower.

## Trip report

### September 22th - From Seoul to Chuncheon

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260922_080444.jpg)
*The cycle ways in and out of Seoul are wonderful for riding, and training.*

My trip started off in Seoul, and the goal was to reach the East coast in three days.
Out of Seoul on their wonderful bike infrastructure was a breeze.
I even ended up cycling some kilometers with a French couple that was on a multi-month trip.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260922_180251.jpg)
*Fifty or so stamps again.*

I hadn't planned to finish the Bukhangang bicyle path on the first day, but I couldn't find a good place to sleep, and my legs were in good shape.
So I made good speed the first day.

It always takes a couple of nights before I'm comfortable wild camping in a new country, so I had planned for campgrounds the first nights.
The first night I ended up on a small hotel, where I could bring my bike into the room.
The bed didn't look super clean, so I ended up sleeping in my sleeping bag.
So a small reminder for sleeping only in hotels is to bring a liner, in case the bed isn't as clean as you prefer.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260922_180532.jpg)
*It was fascinating with the contrast between high rises and the green mountains behind them.*

### September 23th - To the geographical midpoint of Korea

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260923_091734.jpg)
*Starting the day with an interesting cloud formation.*

Looking at the map, I didn't get as far as yesterday.
Not because I didn't cover many miles, but because it was up and down hills, and in and out of valleys.

The weather was great today, reaching 30+ degrees.
I got to know how steep the hills are in Korea.
Instead of snaking up a steep hill, the road just went straight up!
My original plan was to go over the mountains on forest roads, but with the heat and the distance I wanted to cover, I soon realized it was better to stick to the tarmac.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260923_191831.jpg)
*The actual geographical midpoint of Korea!*

I also had some trouble eating and drinking enough.
There were fewer supply points than I had thought.
Guess I've been spoiled with the vending machines in Japan.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260924_064637.jpg)
*Maybe no surprise, but my tent was trenched the next morning.*

On my second night I learned that some campgrounds need to be pre-booked if you want to stay there.
Luckily they allowed me to take a shower.
After scouting around, I found a nice spot down by the river.
Next to a fisherman with a small bonfire.

### September 24th - Found the east coast

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260924_095358.jpg)
*No turning back when you're nearly at the top.*

This morning I continued a trend by eating a microwave burger for breakfast at the convenience store.
It's not the best tasting burgers, but filled my stomach, and it's a lot better than eating the white bread that I usually do in Japan.

I finally tried the mountain roads today, and it was steep, both up and down.
It took a lot longer than I had anticipated.
Because of the uneven terrain, it didn't go that much faster on the way down.
I still had fun, and I'm glad I at least had one day on the mountain roads.
If I had a lot more time I would probably have done some more off-roading, but the coast with its stamps were calling me!

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260924_180758.jpg)
*Finally at the coast, next stop would be Tsushima, Japan.*

I was curious how it would be to cycle on trafficked roads, but it felt like any modern country.
A few cars were a bit too close, but most cars gave me the space I needed.
So all the warnings I've read on the internet were, for me, not true.


### September 25th - Heading south

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260925_100246.jpg)
*Here, but not further.*

When I got packed the tent today I was hoping to be able to catch a ride further north to see the border to North Korea.
Sadly, as I knew, you have to be in a car or a bus, and there was no tour buses going from the Unification Observatory.
So instead I rode as far north as I could manage.
Not sure if it was worth the time, it was mostly farm land, and then the checkpoint you can see in the image above.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260925_112846.jpg)
*The weather didn't look good over the mountains, luckily that was towards Seoul, not Busan.*

After ringing the peace bell, and tasting Omija tea for the first time, really good, I started cycling south.

I ended the day eating pizza at a Surf shop, and borrowing their showers.
It's so much better to go to bed cleaned by water, than wet wipes.

### September 26th - Finally washing my clothes

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260926_105719.jpg)
*One of the coolest buildings I saw in Korea*

I've been riding on average 100km a day, so my legs are getting a bit tired.
So tonight I'll stay at an apartment hotel, for some good rest, and to wash my clothes.

The big happening today was a clock museum, with the history of clocks, and a bunch of cool clocks.
To make it even more interesting, the whole museum was built inside a train!

### September 27th - My seventh day in Korea

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260927_085244.jpg)
*Fascinating how nature can make these formations.*

I started the day with visiting a limestone cave (called Cheongok Golden Bat Cave).
Since I was there a minute before they open, I was the only one walking around inside, and I didn't see anyone else before I came out.
It felt a bit haunted, walking around down in the cave alone.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260927_104357.jpg)
*The weather was really nice today, and three people, helped me take photos at different spots.*

Before traveling to Korea I read about wild camping, and how it is illegal.
After being here a week, I feel like that may not be a law that is followed.
There are so many Koreans camping by the beach, or next to the river.
I also saw (what I assume is) free public campgrounds.
They usually have platforms to pitch your tent on, and toilets.
So now I'm unsure what the rule actually is.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260927_185700.jpg)
*The sun setting over the city of Uljin.*

### September 28th - Finished the east coast

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260928_100847.jpg)
*There are some cool statues here and there along the road.*

Today is the last day of the East coast.
It has been a great route, a lot more up and down than I had anticipated.
The view has been beautiful, easy to find camp spots, and plenty of restaurants.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260928_155505.jpg)
*The last stamp of the East coast, next stamp is in Busan.*

After collecting the last stamp it was time to find out how to get to Busan.
Originally I had planned to keep on cycling, but I was tired, and didn't want to end up in Busan going into the weekend.
So instead I cycled to the closest city.
First I tried to catch a train, but I wasn't allowed on with an unpacked bike of this size.
Sadly I had left my rinko bag at home in Norway.
So then I headed over to the bus terminal.
I had checked the map earlier that day, and it was supposed to be several buses before I'll be in Busan.
Luckily it turned out to just be two buses, and the first bus left in 20 minutes after I arrived, so easy!

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260928_164606.jpg)
*Couldn't take the train from this city, but the bus solved my problems.*

Sadly the bus only to me to the edge of Busan.
Again I tried to take the metro, but it was not allowed with a bike in the weekdays.
So supposedly on Saturdays.
Luckily, I had my bike, so I ended up cycling to my hotel.
It was a fun experience, took me an hour, and I got to know the late evening traffic.
It wasn't bad at all.

### September 29th to 31th - Exploring Busan

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260929_143615.jpg)
*Gamcheon was a colorful experience, I'm glad I'm visiting Korea in the off season.*

The plan for Busan was to relax, and to see what the city has to offer.
I'm not much of a city boy, but I try to see what's recommended.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260929_193257.jpg)
*I was lucky with the date, so I got to see a fountain show!.*

Busan is a wide city, so the first day was spent on the east side, while the second day was spent on the west side.
I got to visit the newly renovated Busan Museum Of Modern Art, which had a great exhibition on artist from the 1945 to 1953 period.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/09/20260930_155416.jpg)
*I wanted to ride these cute wagons, but they were all booked out for the day.*

### October 1th - Incheon next

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261001_091636.jpg)
*Bukangi the shark!*

I started the day by riding down to the harbor to see the wild shark that had strayed into the northern harbor in Busan.
It had been there for some days, and I was lucky to read about it on Facebook the night before.
So I had to see it!
First time seeing a shark in "the wild".

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261001_130048.jpg)
*It was the perfect time to see flowers in bloom!*

Riding out of Busan was quite monotone, and fewer places to stop than I had anticipated.
I also had some headwind, which didn't help on my mood.

### October 2th - One beautiful sunset

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261002_110049.jpg)
*More flat roads, going mostly straight.*

It was mostly just riding today, with the river on one side, and rice fields on the other.
Not as easy to find places to eat, as on the East coast, as most restaurants are near the car roads.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261002_144357.jpg)
*Nearly all hills had beautiful view of the river.*

I ended the day camping by the river.
I had originally looked at a place under a bridge, but there was an outdoor sculpture park next to it, with a bit too many people.
So instead I ended up riding 30 minutes more, and had one of my best nights sleeping in a tent.
Either I was really tired, of the spot was perfect.
	
![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261002_182412.jpg)
*Some cool art, with a nice sunset in the background.*

### October 3th - Got myself some devil horns

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261003_061831.jpg)
*The first sunrise I saw on the trip, I'm a late sleeper.*

Perfect month to dress up a bit.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261003_154434.jpg)

It was a social day, more than what I'm used to.
I even ended up relaxing in the sleep stream of three guys doing the route in just three days.

### October 4th - Mechanical breakdowns

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261004_073702.jpg)
*The weather was perfect today, with a nearly clear sky.*

Today's goal was the Andong dam, a stamp all by itself, 70km from the main route.
Not part of the cross-country route, but it was the best time to get it.
Don't want to back track on a later trip to Korea.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261004_094145.jpg)
*There are too few masks in Norwegian culture.*

On my way I stopped by a folk village, where you could walk, or bike around.
It was a bit strange to look at the old houses, while people still lived in them.
Felt a little bit like a human zoo.
It was also a clash between the new and the old, with electric cars parked outside, and air condition mounted on the walls.

I also visited a mask museum, that have masks from all around the world.
People make some really interesting masks!

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261004_142837.jpg)
*Over a thousand year old stone pagoda.*

As the title suggested, I had my first real mechanical breakdown on this trip.
I've been cycling for several years now, and this is the first time I've had a proper failure on any of my bikes.
While gearing down coming over a hill top, something snapped, and my gearing stopped working.
Luckily I wasn't far from Andong, so I hoped to find a mechanic there.

After searching on Naver I found one, and he had some bad news.
The spring inside the handle had broken, so no gearing on the rear derailleur.
He didn't have a new handle, so there was nothing to be done in Andong.
So it's going to be an interesting ride towards Seoul.

### October 5th - Living the life with two gears

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261005_130950.jpg)
*Fall was coming to Korea, with beautiful colored leafs.*

Cycling with just one gear turned out to be less of a problem than I had thought.
I had to walk some of the hills, but the straights made me go a bit faster than I usually do.
So I got a steady rhythm.

Met several cyclists, one of whom I think lived on the bike.
He had a lot of gear.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261005_152801.jpg)
*I felt I had a good rhythm, before this guy sped past me.*

I also met a guy from California, that had started his trip in Sapporo, cycling on a fixed-gear!
That's nuts, if you ask me.
I ended up first joining him in the only onsen/public bath I visited on the whole trip.
It was really nice soaking in the warm water, and getting a proper scrub.
Afterwards we had dinner, and we ended up sharing a hotel.

I had originally planned to tent, but hadn't seen any good spots, and a couple that came the other way hadn't seen any good spot either, so they were going to continue to find one.
So sharing a hotel was a good solution, it was also a bit chilly this night.

### October 6th - The biggest hill on the cross-country

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261006_070418.jpg)
*The sun rising over the Joryeongsan mountains.*

I got up early this morning, so I didn't get to say goodbye to my roommate, but it was fun hanging out!

It was quite chilly this morning, so I was wearing most of my clothes.
The day also started with the "biggest" hill on the cross-country route, so I had to manually shift my gear down.
Since it was only the spring in the handle, all I had to do was tighten the wire that hold the derailleur to shift it up, or down.
Not much of a problem, just takes some time to find the right spot.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261006_083444.jpg)
*I had gotten a bit warmer on the top, just one more small hill left.*

Maybe the day's highlight was finding real Norwegian brown cheese at a café.
While doing the second side quest/stamp on the cross-country route, I stopped at Café 198510 to get something to eat.
Turns out they served waffles and ice cream, with shredded brown cheese.
Never thought of shredding my brown cheese before, but that was a great way to eat it on ice cream!

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261006_174446.jpg)
*I experienced so many golden sunsets in Korea!*

### October 7th - Second to last day

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261007_094250.jpg)
*If you like dam infrastructure, Korea is the place for you!*

It was hard getting going today, and I ended up with a delayed lunch, which didn't make it any easier.
While looking for a place to eat lunch I met a Korean cyclist that had seen me yesterday, and after a bit of chatting, we ended up going to a lunch place together.

Not sure if it was me being hungry, but this was one of the best lunches I had in Korea.
So if you are near, I can recommend the little restaurant 조안하우스.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261007_162415.jpg)
*While getting my last stamp of the day, I met a fellow Norwegian!*

I wasn't comfortable tenting inside the borders of Seoul, so I decided to go for a hotel this night too.
Three nights with hotels felt a bit too much, so was hoping that at least the last night would be spent in my tent.

### October 8th - Finished the Cross Country route

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261008_093527.jpg)
*The last stamp of the route, and for this year's trip.*

Cycling through Seoul is flat and monotone.
So the last stretch was not the most entertaining, but I did it, I crossed the finish line at the harbor in Incheon!
As planned, I bought the medallion to put on my shelf when I got home.

My trip wasn't completely over, as I had one more night before my wife would arrive from Norway.
So it was time to see the North Korean border!

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261008_152117.jpg)
*This little town was just 2.3km from me, on the other side of the river.*

Cycling a couple of hours north, to the Ganghwa Peace Observatory, one of the closest to the border.
I had to go through two checkpoints on the way to the observatory, but only one on the way back.
So I guess the checkpoints depends on which road you take.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261008_161738.jpg)
*The whole South Korean island was surrounded by barbed wires.*

It was kinda surreal to look over at North Korea.
So similar to the south, rice fields, small houses, people tilling the earth.
The one difference, that I could see, was the lack of cars.
When I've been cycling around South Korea, there have always been cars parked near the fields, but in North Korea I only saw some farm tractors, and a moped.

Using the binoculars on the observatory I got into a kind of trance, just watching people move around, kinda hoping something abnormal would happen.
Nothing did.

### October 9th - It's been a great trip

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261009_075407.jpg)
*Paying ₩40 000 felt a bit too much for camping on gravel.*

Since I passed through three checkpoints yesterday, and it was farmland nearly all over the place, I decided to go for my first traditional campground.
It was not the best, and a bit too expensive, but I got a shower, and one final night in my tent.

![](https://kyrremann-plog.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/images/2026/10/20261009_084319.jpg)

Korea didn't have as many souvenirs to buy as Japan, but I managed to gather some!

On the way back to my hotel in Seoul I met a lot of cyclists cycling out to the island.
The weather was beautiful, so perfect for long day in the saddle.
